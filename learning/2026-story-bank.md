## EXAMPLE Story: Microservices Migration Decision — DO NOT USE

**Tags:** `technical` `architecture` `leadership` `stakeholder`  
**Questions it answers:** Q1, Q8, Q11, Q23

### STARR

- **Situation:** Our e-commerce platform was struggling with a monolithic architecture serving 2M daily users. Deployment cycles took 3 weeks, and a single bug could bring down the entire system.

- **Task:** I was tasked with leading the technical assessment of whether we should migrate to microservices, considering we had a major product launch in 6 months.

- **Actions:**
  1. I spearheaded a thorough cost-benefit analysis, involving 3 senior engineers and 2 product managers over 2 weeks
  2. I orchestrated a series of stakeholder meetings to align business and technical perspectives on acceptable risks
  3. I proposed a hybrid approach: extracting 3 critical services first while keeping the core monolith, reducing risk while proving the concept

- **Result:** We reduced deployment time from 3 weeks to 2 days for extracted services. The hybrid approach saved an estimated $200K in migration costs. Zero downtime during the transition, and we hit the product launch deadline.

- **Reflection:** In retrospect, I learned that the best technical decisions often involve finding a middle ground. This experience shaped my approach to always seek incremental migration paths rather than big-bang rewrites.

### Key Phrases
- **"I spearheaded a thorough cost-benefit analysis"** — when describing leadership in technical evaluation
- **"I orchestrated a series of stakeholder meetings"** — when describing coordination efforts
- **"The tradeoff ultimately came down to..."** — when narrowing to key decision factors
- **"In retrospect, I learned that..."** — when reflecting on lessons
